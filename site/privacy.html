Revv for Android

Privacy policy

Effective 9 October 2026

In short
  • Revv has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and there is no Revv server.
  • Your phone's calls and contacts, your car's readings and the camera picture stay on the head unit.
  • To show maps, plan routes and search for places, Revv asks public OpenStreetMap services, which see the map area, the route's start and end, or what you searched for.
  • Nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

This policy covers the Revv app ("Revv", "the app") for Android head units, made by Vivek Kaushik. It explains what the app reads, what it keeps on the head unit, and what leaves it.

What Revv reads, and why

Your phone, over Bluetooth

When you pair a phone with the head unit and allow it to share contacts and call history, Revv reads the phone's call history, favourites and contacts (names and phone numbers) over Bluetooth, the way a car kit does. It uses them to show recent calls, favourites and the contact list, and to find people as you type in the dialer.

When you call someone from Revv, it asks the phone over Bluetooth to dial the number, and follows the call so it can show how it is going and let you end it.

The calls and contacts are held in memory only and are read again from the phone when needed. Revv remembers just which phone it read last (its Bluetooth address), so it can read the same phone next time. It also reads the phone's name, battery level and connection state to show them.

Your car, over OBD-II

If you set up an OBD-II adapter, Revv reads vehicle data from it: speed, engine speed, engine load, throttle, temperatures, fuel level, battery voltage, the check-engine light and fault codes. It uses them for the gauges, the gear indicator, trip figures and warnings. If you ask, it also tells the car to clear its fault codes.

Revv keeps the gear ratios it learns and the protocol each adapter uses. If "Save adapter logs" is on, it writes one log file a day of what the adapter replied, for troubleshooting, and keeps the latest 14 days.

Location

With your permission, Revv uses the head unit's location to show the car on the map, give turn-by-turn directions and rank nearby search results. For dimming the screen at sunset, it keeps an approximate position, rounded to about 10 km, to work out sunset on the head unit itself.

Camera

The Rear Cam screen shows the head unit's camera live, with your permission. The picture is shown only while that screen is open and is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

Media and notifications

If you allow media access (Android's notification access), Revv sees what your music or media app is playing so it can show it and control playback. It does not read, keep or send other notifications.

Screen brightness and system settings

If you allow it, Revv changes the screen's brightness and adaptive-brightness setting from its Display settings and for night dimming. With the car-hotspot link for wireless CarPlay, the same permission lets Revv turn the head unit's Wi-Fi hotspot on.

CarPlay

CarPlay is a direct link between the head unit and your iPhone, over USB or the head unit's own Wi-Fi (a Wi-Fi Direct group, or the car's hotspot). The iPhone sends its screen and sound; Revv sends back your touches, the microphone while you use Siri or take a call through CarPlay, and, if you turn Location to iPhone on, the head unit's position for the iPhone's maps. Over USB, Android's VPN interface carries the iPhone's link on the head unit itself; no traffic goes to the internet through it.

CarPlay needs an Apple accessory identity (a private key and its certificate) that you import yourself. Revv keeps it, and the pairing records the iPhone and head unit agree on, in its private storage on the head unit, out of Android's backups, and never sends them anywhere. CarPlay's route guidance (the destination's name and the next turn) is used to show the turn on Revv's map and, if you ask, to route Revv's own map there with the place search described below.

If you save a diagnostic report from Settings › CarPlay, it is written to the head unit's Downloads folder for you to read or share; Revv does not send it.

What leaves the head unit

Revv sends nothing to its developer. To show maps, it uses these public services over the internet, which, like any website, also see the head unit's IP address:

ServiceUsed forWhat it receives
OpenFreeMapMap tiles and map letteringRequests for the parts of the map on screen, which show roughly where the map is looking
Valhalla on openstreetmap.de (FOSSGIS)Planning routesThe route's start (your position and heading), its destination, and whether to avoid tolls
Photon (komoot)Searching for placesWhat you type in the search, and your approximate position to rank results nearby

These services are run by others under their own privacy policies. Revv does not send them your name, phone contacts, call history, vehicle data or camera picture.

Bluetooth and Wi-Fi connections to your phone and OBD-II adapter, and CarPlay's USB or Wi-Fi link to your iPhone, are direct, local links; they do not go through the internet.

What is kept on the head unit

  • Your settings, including the car's details, the chosen adapter and apps for the fuel widget and projection
  • Up to six recent places you navigated to
  • The Bluetooth address of the phone read last
  • Learnt gear ratios and adapter protocols
  • An approximate position for sunset times, rounded to about 10 km
  • Adapter logs for up to 14 days, if "Save adapter logs" is on
  • Your CarPlay identity, the iPhone pairing records, and CarPlay's settings, including the car hotspot's name and password if you save them
  • A CarPlay diagnostic report in Downloads, only when you save one

All of it stays in Revv's own storage on the head unit. If the head unit's Android backup is turned on, Android may include Revv's settings and recent places in that backup, under the terms of the backup service you use.

You can refuse or withdraw any of these in Android's settings; the matching feature then stops working and the rest of Revv carries on.

Your choices

  • Clear recent places from Settings › Navigation.
  • Turn off "Save adapter logs" in Settings › Vehicle.
  • Stop sharing contacts and call history in the phone's Bluetooth settings for the head unit.
  • Clear Revv's storage in Android's app settings, or uninstall Revv, to remove everything it keeps.

Children

Revv is a car launcher meant for drivers and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If Revv starts handling data differently, this page will be updated and its effective date changed. Significant changes will also be noted in the release notes.

Contact

Questions about privacy or this policy can be raised on Revv's GitHub issues.
